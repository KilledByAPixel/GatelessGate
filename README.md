# The Gateless Gate

*An interactive sumi-e reading of the Mumonkan, in your browser.*

The **Mumonkan** — "The Gateless Gate" — is a thirteenth-century collection of
forty-eight Zen koans: small, strange stories a student sits with until something
opens. This is that book, reimagined as a quiet place you can wander rather than
pages you turn.

Each koan is staged as a low-poly ink-painting diorama — a monk on a hillside, a
flag in the wind, a cat, a candle, an oak tree in a garden — drawn in black ink on
paper with a single color of accent, and dissolving into fog before it ever reaches
a horizon. You read the case, its old master's commentary, and its closing verse;
you can have them **read aloud**; and a bell, some wind, or the quiet knock of a
wooden fish keeps the scene company. Nothing is asked of you. There are no puzzles
and no score — it is a book, not a game.

## What's inside

- **All forty-nine cases** — Mumon's forty-eight and Amban's traditional addition —
  each with its own scene, the full text, and a spoken reading.
- **Touch, if you like.** Scenes answer a little to a cursor or a tap — a flag
  ruffles and stills, water rings, a bell sounds — small delights you stumble on,
  never something you have to find.
- **Ambient sound** that is gentle by design: wind, bells, the occasional chime,
  tuned quiet. No chanting, no singing bowl, nothing loud by default.
- **Sit for a while.** From any case you can start a short meditation timer — the
  scene settles, the text fades, a bell opens and closes the sitting, and the case
  keeps a small vermillion mark once you've sat with it.
- **Read in any order.** A simple menu lists every case; there's no path to follow
  and nothing to unlock.

It runs entirely in the browser — no install, no sign-in, nothing downloaded up
front. Just open it and read.

## The text

The reading is the Nyogen Senzaki and Paul Reps English rendering, privately printed
in Los Angeles in 1934 and in the US public domain, lightly modernized where the old
verse forms had gone archaic. The aim is a thing beautiful enough to draw in someone
who has never heard of a koan, and faithful enough to reward someone who has.

## Running it yourself

It's a static site — no build step. From the project folder:

    npx -y http-server -p 8105 -c-1 .

then open <http://localhost:8105>.
