fūrin swing (SWING, kit/furin.js)

tap kick, settle time, and swing cap — the three PROBLEM 2 opened up. live: takes effect on the very next tap / next frame, no reload.

fūrin ring-down (CLAP_FORCE, kit/furin.js)

the clapper is a second pendulum now, so a tap rings a cluster and keeps ringing as the swing settles. "full-force at" is measured in units of this chime's OWN full-force tap velocity, so it holds across sizes; "stops below" is where a contact gets too soft to speak — that is what ends the ring-down (without it: 32 contacts, 81 strikes, still going 15s later).

tanzaku spin (SPIN, kit/furin.js)

the paper strip below a SINGLE turns on its own thread — a torsional pendulum, so a hard enough knock carries it over the top and spinning before it settles back to a twist. Where that happens is well above the undamped 2·√stiffness: at the shipped stiffness a full tap turns 0.4 of a revolution at kick 6 and 1.4 at kick 10, because the strip loses better than half its energy on the way up. Rings are untouched — their tag keeps the flutter it has.

cylinder swing (CYL_SWING, kit/cylinder.js)

tap kick swings the BODY (not the clapper) — raising it swings it further AND makes the ring-down last longer (more re-strikes at the body's own ~0.78s half-period, unrelated to amplitude); the force law is what makes those re-strikes fade instead of hammering at full volume, so a bigger kick should still sound like one settling ring, not a louder loop.

cylinder ring-down (CYL_FORCE, kit/cylinder.js)

these two ARE the diminuendo — starting points, not audited by ear. "knee level" is how loud a strike gets before it's past wind's own range (raise it and typical wind knocks get louder, taps start closer to full volume already); "cap (rad/s)" is how hard a contact has to be to report full force — lower it and a tap's ring-down saturates sooner (fewer strikes taper, more sit near max); raise it and the tail fades further/faster. Live: the very next strike hears it, no reload.
